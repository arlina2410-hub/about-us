title: Cartography of Ordinary Days
date: 2025-12-20
category: Reflections
tags: journey, time, stillness
---
First comes the waking before the chime,
the gray wash across the bedroom floor,
the quiet counting of the breath
before another day begins.

Then the walk down the narrow stair,
each worn tread known to the foot,
the creak that tells the empty hall
someone is alive inside the house.

By noon the light is flat and clear,
revealing dust upon the shelf,
the unread letters stacked like tile,
the tea gone cold inside the cup.

We speak of small, practical things—
the postal run, the coming rain,
the hinges that require oil,
the coat that lost its bottom button.

Yet under every simple chore
runs the deep channel of our years,
unmeasured by the kitchen clock,
unhurried by the winter wind.

We take the evening by the sleeve
and draw it close around our shoulders,
content with bread and simmered broth
and light that softens toward the west.

No map was drawn for where we go,
yet every turning seems familiar,
as if we walked this road before
in dreams we forgot upon waking.

Here let the ledger balance out:
we lived another daylight through,
we kept the promise of the roof,
we gave the fire wood to burn.
